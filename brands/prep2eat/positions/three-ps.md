# Three P's — Prep2Eat

> Positioning groundwork. Source: `business-overview.md`, `audience.md`
> (from repo `the Prep2Eat app repo` docs, enriched 2026-07-07).
> App is in testing — no real-user data yet; mark unknowns.

## Person
**Who, specifically, feels the pain?**
- **Archetype / role:** Home cooks and food enthusiasts — busy people who want
  fast answers to "what's for dinner?"
- Demographics: age 13+, per store listing. `NEEDS OWNER INPUT` on deliberate
  secondary segments (families? fitness/health eaters? budget shoppers?).
- What they care about: saving time, reducing meal-prep stress, using what's
  already in the fridge.

## Pain
- Core problem: "What's in my fridge?" paralysis — turning available ingredients
  into a recipe fast.
- Recipe chaos — recipes scattered across sites, screenshots, saved videos.
- Meal-plan-to-shopping friction — hand-making lists.
- How they feel: decision fatigue at meal time, overwhelmed by scattered recipes.
- Tried already: manual meal planning, saved videos, note apps (context clues).

## Promise
- The offer: AI-powered recipe generator, meal planner, and smart grocery list
  in one app. "AI recipes. Real plans." Turns ingredients into a weekly plan in
  under a minute.
- Outcome: planned week of meals, grocery list auto-done, one recipe library.
- Why credible: privacy-first (no ads, no tracking, no data selling), free core
  features — answers "another subscription" objection.
- Differentiator: social-video recipe import (Instagram/TikTok/YouTube) — strong
  for the short-form target.

## Positioning Statement (draft)
> For busy home cooks facing "what's in my fridge?" paralysis, **Prep2Eat** is
> the all-in-one recipe + meal-plan app that turns what you already have into a
> planned week — with the shopping list done.
