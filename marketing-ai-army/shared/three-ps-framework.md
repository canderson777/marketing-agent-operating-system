# Three P's Framework — Brand Position

Every business, brand, and incoming client must define the Three P's **before**
any marketing, content, or asset work starts. Without this, output looks and
feels like generic AI slop — no purpose, no intent.

Source: adapted from the "one-person marketing team" video (Claude + Higgs Field,
38:30). This framework is the required foundation of the `brand-positioning.md`
doc each brand folder carries.

## The Three P's

| P | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Who, specifically, feels the pain? (A real, single archetype — not "everyone") |
| **Pain** | What is the problem your business is actually trying to solve? |
| **Promise** | How does your brand/product/service promise to solve that pain for that person? |

## Why this matters

- Assets, copy, ads, websites, videos, and scripts must not be "AI slop."
- Every creative has to have **purpose and intent**, grounded in a real person,
  a real pain, and a credible promise.
- The three P's feed **everything downstream**:
  - brand positioning doc / positioning statement
  - message bank, voice & copy, playbooks
  - websites (copy + structure must appeal to the target person)
  - ad creatives, hooks, scripts, video assets
  - images, logos, thumbnails, product shots

## When to fill it out

- New business / client / project intake — **mandatory** before assets.
- When an existing brand's offer, audience, or positioning shifts.
- When a claim or creative references a pain the brand hasn't actually verified.

## How to fill it

Answer one line per P in plain language. Then expand with evidence from the
brand's own files (audience.md, offers.md, business-overview.md, archived
posts, real customer conversations). **Never invent** pain, person, or promise —
mark unknown areas `NEEDS OWNER INPUT`.

## Positioning statement (optional extension)

For brands that want a one-line promise, use this shape:

> For **[person]** who **[pain]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that **[promise]**.

The three P's are the raw material; the positioning statement is the short-form
version used in ads, site H1s, and hooks.
